## Definition (`eau-srh`)

"Persistent inability to attain and maintain an erection sufficient to permit satisfactory sexual
performance."

## How common (`eau-srh`, epidemiology chapter)

- Massachusetts Male Aging Study: "overall ED prevalence of 52% in non-institutionalised men aged
  40-70 years in the Boston area"; minimal, moderate and complete ED in 17.2%, 25.2% and 9.6%.
- Cologne study (men aged 30–80): prevalence 19.2%, "with a steep age-related increase from 2.3%
  to 53.4%".
- A real-life clinical study: "one in four patients was younger than 40 years, with almost 50% of
  the young men complaining of severe ED".
  Use these with their study names and populations. Do not turn them into "X% of Romanian men".

## Causes and risk factors (`eau-srh`)

- Categories: "vasculogenic, neurogenic, anatomical, hormonal, drug-induced and/or psychogenic".
- Risk factors: age, diabetes, dyslipidaemia, hypertension, cardiovascular disease, obesity,
  metabolic syndrome, smoking, lack of exercise, depression, anxiety disorders, chronic kidney
  disease, COPD, sleep disorders, COVID-19 infection.
- Medicines and substances that can cause ED: antihypertensives (thiazide diuretics,
  beta-blockers); antidepressants (SSRIs, tricyclics); antipsychotics; antiandrogens (GnRH
  analogues and antagonists, 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors); recreational drugs (heroin, cocaine,
  marijuana, methadone, synthetic drugs, anabolic steroids, excessive alcohol).
- Site rule: tell readers never to stop a prescribed medicine on their own; talk to the
  prescribing doctor.

## ED and heart health (`eau-srh`) — /disfunctie-erectila/sanatatea-inimii

- "Erectile dysfunction significantly increases the risk of CVD, coronary heart disease, stroke,
  atrial fibrillation, CV and all-cause mortality."
- "ED should be considered a precursor of CVD, and more severe and longer standing ED yields
  greater risk." Younger men (especially under 50) with ED have a higher cardiovascular risk.
- The guideline gives no specific time interval between ED onset and cardiovascular events. Do not
  write one.
- Cardiovascular risk is assessed with a risk score (Princeton Consensus IV uses the ACC/AHA ASCVD
  score: age, sex, ethnicity, cholesterol, blood pressure, smoking); coronary calcium scoring can
  be considered in borderline to intermediate risk.
- Men with "severe cardiac conditions or unstable enough to pose a significant risk with sexual
  activity" need a cardiac evaluation before treatment.
- "No RCTs or open-label studies have demonstrated an increase in myocardial infarction (MI) rates
  in patients receiving PDE5Is."

## Diagnosis (`eau-srh`, strong recommendations)

- "Take a comprehensive medical and sexual history in every patient presenting with erectile
  dysfunction", including rigidity and duration of sexually stimulated and morning erections.
- "Use a validated questionnaire related to ED" (e.g. IIEF). Site rule: Telegen does not reproduce
  IIEF-5 (licence not verified).
- "Include a focused physical examination in the initial assessment": genitourinary, endocrine,
  vascular and neurological; blood pressure, heart rate, BMI/waist.
- "Evaluate laboratory tests, including glucose and lipid profile and total testosterone": fasting
  glucose or HbA1c (if not done in the last 12 months), lipid profile, early-morning fasting total
  testosterone; PSA, prolactin, LH in selected patients.
- Specialist testing is indicated for: primary ED; young men with pelvic or perineal trauma; penile
  deformity (e.g. Peyronie's disease, congenital curvature); complex psychiatric or psychosexual
  disorders; complex endocrine disorders. These match the existing ED hard stops (onset after
  injury, curvature or pain).

## Treatment (`eau-srh`)

- Strong: "Initiate lifestyle changes and risk factor modification prior to, or at the same time
  as, initiating erectile dysfunction (ED) treatments." Physical activity (especially aerobic),
  weight loss, treating cardiovascular risk factors "may improve sexual function".
- Strong: PDE5 inhibitors as first-line therapy.

  | sildenafil | 30–60 min               | up to 12 hours     |
  | tadalafil  | 30 min (peak about 2 h) | up to 36 hours     |
  | vardenafil | 30 min                  | —                  |
  | avanafil   | 15–30 min               | —                  |

- Common PDE5 inhibitor side effects: headache, flushing, dyspepsia, nasal congestion; back pain
  and muscle pain with tadalafil.
- Strong: psychosexual therapy (CBT, sexual skills training, couple therapy, psychosexual
  education); combining CBT with medical treatment is described as the best-practice approach.
- Other options, all handled by specialists: vacuum erection devices; intracavernous injections
  (alprostadil, strong); low-intensity shockwave therapy (weak, modest benefit in mild vasculogenic
  ED); penile prosthesis "if other treatments fail or depending upon patient preference" (strong).
  Describe them briefly as specialist options; Telegen does not provide them.
- The guideline gives efficacy percentages (e.g. sildenafil 56–84% vs 25% placebo). Do NOT print
  efficacy percentages on medicine pages until the lawyer answers question 7 in

## Sildenafil (`ema-viagra`, Viagra SmPC)

- "The medicine can only be obtained with a prescription." (Romania: PRF, see
  romania-prescription-status.md.)
- How it works: a PDE5 inhibitor; it blocks the enzyme that breaks down cGMP, allowing blood flow
  into the penis during sexual stimulation. "In order for VIAGRA to be effective, sexual
  stimulation is required."
- Taken as needed about one hour before sexual activity; "the maximum recommended dosing frequency
  is once a day". Median onset about 25 minutes in fasted patients (SmPC). No doses on the site.
- Must not be taken with nitrates (including recreational amyl nitrite, per EAU) or riociguat; not
  in severe heart disease, unstable angina, severe heart failure, recent stroke or heart attack,
  severe low blood pressure (below 90/50 mmHg), severe liver disease, previous NAION (sudden loss of
  vision from poor blood flow to the optic nerve), or hereditary retinal disease such as retinitis
  pigmentosa.
- Common side effects: headache, flushing, indigestion, visual disturbances, nasal congestion,
  dizziness, nausea, hot flushes.
- Alpha-blockers: "Caution is advised … as the co-administration may lead to symptomatic
  hypotension in a few susceptible individuals." (See conflicts-and-decisions.md.)
- Grapefruit juice can modestly raise blood levels.
- Not for anyone under 18.
- "Prior to initiating any treatment for erectile dysfunction, physicians should consider the
  cardiovascular status of their patients."

## Tadalafil (`ema-cialis`)

- "The medicine can only be obtained with a prescription." (Romania: PRF.)
- Indicated for ED and for symptoms of benign prostatic hyperplasia. Sexual stimulation is
  required.
- Used either on demand (at least 30 minutes before sexual activity) or as a low daily dose for
  men with frequent sexual activity (twice a week or more). Effect up to 36 hours (EAU). No doses
  on the site.
- Must not be used with nitrates or riociguat, or after "a heart attack within the last three
  months or a stroke within the last six months", or with uncontrolled cardiovascular conditions.
- Common side effects: headache, indigestion, back pain and muscle pain, more frequent at higher
  doses.
